# Next Generation Technology Group (319A)

- **Posted:** 2026-11-12
- **Source:** https://www.valueinvestorsclub.com/idea/Next_Generation_Technology_Group/2761071256#messages

## Description

**Company overview**

Next Generation Technology Group (NGTG) is a serial acquirer of specialist manufacturing and industrial businesses. It generally buys 100% of a company and leaves operating decisions with local managers while offering succession planning, recruitment, procurement and production systems from the HQ.

Since inception in 2018, the company has acquired over 20 companies with an accelerating pace after its IPO in Feb 2025. Although it relies on business brokers as the main funnel for deals (as compared to proprietary networks for successful serial acquirers in Sweden or the US), it is extremely stringent in screening deal flow, acquiring 0.7% of deals considered.

The company isn't cheap by any metric, but I believe they will be able to compound EBITDA at 20%+ for the next 5-8 years due to the reasons stated below and the stock price should follow suit.

**Thesis 1: NGTG wins deals even though they are never the highest bidder**

Arai (CEO) said in most of their deals, they are the lowest bidder. In Japan, due to cultural reasons, maintaining a brand and ensuring remaining employees are taken care of can be more important than a few million dollars.

When they approach a company, they attach an operating and succesion plan to its offer. According to testimonials, acquired companies describe retaining freedom, continuity for employees and customers, and grooming successors from middle management.

Arai promises sellers that NGTG will not sell a company (although it isn't contractual) which appeals to many owners who are wary of strategic buyers or PEs who seek synergies with a larger platform.

**Thesis 2: Repeatable playbook**

Before founding NGTG, Arai took a year to travel the world and study successful serial acquirers. He settled on Danaher (because it was the largest company by market cap - silly reason imo), and decided to have a playbook similar to them called NGP (NGTG Growth Program).

Based on our conversations with the company, they estimate that NGP typically leads to a one-off 200-300bps uplift in EBITDA margins after 12-18 months. This is significant because their median target company has 10% EBITDA margin which they buy for 2-4x EBITDA. Even at zero organic growth, a 30% increase in profits lowers the multiple to 1.5-3.1x EBITDA, giving them their money back in less than 4 years.

Some examples that were shared with me were introducing lot-level production tracking and repricing jobs using cost data, or simply changing raw material or utility suppliers.

The company also realises that Japanese management might not be familiar with ROIC or FCF yields, so to simplify, they set KPIs on EBITDA margins and EBITDA growth that most general managers of these SMEs understand.

**Thesis 3: Leverage through low cost non-recourse debt**

In most companies, leverage would be something that I avoid like the plague but due to the B2B and recurring nature of the businesses they acquire, NGTG is comfortable borrowing 85-99% LTV, meaning their cash outlay would be 1-15% of the acquisition price. This means that their levered returns could be over 100% in the first year.

Prior to Nov 2025, they were able to get 10-yr fixed rate at 1-1.5% on their loans. Since Q3 2025, they have started using floating rate loans at around 1.5-2%.

In addition, retained cash in the companies they acquire help support a rising acquisition budget. They acquired 7 companies for JPY 9.9bn with JPY 4.6bn in net cash. Subtracting 562m of deal fees, their net cost was 5.9bn.

**Risk 1: Small businesses might have single points of failure**

Aero Craft (a detractor in 2025) made niche composite and metal components for race cars. A delay in F1 development order from Honda reduced sales by 30-40% in that subsidiary. The order eventually came back, but it is telling that a single order can move the needle.

Breaking down their key subsidiaries, Horikoshi supplies machined industrial and semicon parts. Sanko sells PCB processing equipment. Taga makes brake and EV springs, Suntec treats metals for cars and bicycles. Kanda supplies to rail and industrial customers. Toshima has materials for research and batteries.

In that sense, NGTG thinks that while they are exposed to manufacturing companies, the end customer tends to be diversified as a group.

**Risk 2: Rising rates in Japan**

Although many more seasoned and successful macro traders have been betting on the BOJ raising rates to no avail, it is a growing risk for NGTG as their acquisitions in 2026 have been funded by floating rate debt.

At 2%, even if the company was funded 99% by debt, assuming 10% EBITDA margins, that shaves off 20% from profits. However, if rates were to rise rapidly (100-200bps), I expect the company to face pressure and may slow down pace of acquisitions.

As of June 2026, gross debt was JPY 18bn against 12.7bn of cash + ST investments, leaving them with 5.3bn of net debt, equal to 1.33x ND/EBITDA. They are targeting 3-4x ND/EBITDA, meaning they expect at least another 11bn of debt going forward that would be floating.
